# Live Trend Intelligence

## Use only when freshness changes the answer

Run live research when the request says current/latest/2026, asks for platform traffic fit, leaves a competitive direction open, or reaches publication. Skip it for a self-contained rewrite where external trend claims would not change the work.

Trend research informs the creative decision; it does not write the story by popularity vote.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. current official rules, help centers, creator centers, and platform disclosures;
2. first-party trend/search/creative tools such as Douyin Index/巨量算数 or TikTok Creative Center;
3. current public posts and comment surfaces observed directly, with platform, region, language, capture time, and URL;
4. reputable research or industry reporting with disclosed method and date;
5. search summaries, Grok, Doubao, or other model answers as discovery leads only.

Grok and Doubao may broaden Chinese/English discovery or challenge an interpretation. Require them to return original sources. Open the source yourself before promoting a claim. Never cite a model answer as proof and never let a secondary model silently become the final creative author.

If a login-only surface is needed, reuse an already authorized session and perform read-only research. Do not post, follow, like, comment, or change an account during research.

## Maintain a compact evidence ledger

For every claim that changes the creative or publishing decision, retain:

```text
claim_id
claim
evidence_class: official_rule | first_party_signal | observed_sample | industry_observation | inference
source_url
publisher
published_at if available
captured_at
platform / region / language
what was directly observed
uncertainty and expiry condition
decision affected
```

Search snippets are leads. If the original page is missing, paywalled, deleted, or contradictory, mark it unverified. Do not fill gaps from memory.

## Sample until the mechanism stabilizes

Do not hard-code a universal sample count. Gather enough current, varied examples to distinguish a recurring mechanism from one outlier. Diversify across:

- organic versus paid;
- large and smaller creators;
- the target region and language;
- adjacent genres and audiences;
- recent breakout and durable repeat performers;
- successful posts and visible counterexamples when available.

Stop when additional samples repeat the same mechanism or when the evidence limit is clear. More links do not compensate for weak provenance.

## Distill mechanisms, not topics

For each useful cluster, extract only what can change an original work:

- audience situation and existing collective recognition;
- emotional promise and what viewers appear to wait for;
- first-readable-moment mechanism;
- story or visual progression;
- performance, dialogue, sound, camera, and edit texture;
- comment tension: what people disagree about, quote, reinterpret, or ask next;
- novelty distance from saturated versions;
- rights, disclosure, recommendation, and safety risks;
- expected decay: momentary meme, seasonal behavior, or durable human pattern.

Do not copy character combinations, signature lines, distinctive props, shot order, visual identity, or an identifiable creator's expression. Rebuild the human mechanism with new causality and visual truth.

## Separate platform fact from creative inference

Use this language discipline:

- **Official fact:** the platform or regulator states it.
- **First-party signal:** an official trend tool or report observes it, often for a limited market or commercial context.
- **Observed sample:** it was visible in captured posts, but the cause of performance is unknown.
- **Industry observation:** useful but not authoritative.
- **Inference:** our reasoned creative hypothesis.

Never turn correlation into a secret algorithm rule. No “three-second weight,” completion threshold, traffic-pool cutoff, posting-time guarantee, or hashtag formula enters the Skill without current first-party proof.

## Convert evidence into a creative hypothesis

A useful hypothesis states:

```text
For <specific audience and platform context>,
<recognizable human or visual situation>
may earn continuation because <observed mechanism>.
We will express it through <original formal choice>,
while changing <characters / causality / imagery / point of view / consequence>.
The main risk or uncertainty is <x>.
```

Then let the open creative director decide whether to use it. A trend with poor story fit should be rejected.

## Feedback after publication

Use actual account analytics as diagnostic evidence, compared with the same platform, account, format, and similar release stage:

- early skip or hold;
- watch time and completion appropriate to duration;
- rewatch or loop behavior;
- shares, saves, comments, follows, and qualified next action;
- negative feedback, restriction, or eligibility status;
- the timestamp or scene where behavior changes.

Change one meaningful creative variable per test when possible. A metric suggests where to inspect; it does not explain the cause by itself.

## Freshness

Always record capture time. Fast memes and ranking surfaces may decay in days; cultural patterns may last longer; platform rules and provider schemas must be reopened at execution or publication. A dated snapshot may guide planning but cannot authorize a current post.
